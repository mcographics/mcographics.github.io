The words of Yeshua deserve more than being separated into attractive quotations. A single verse can be powerful, but its surrounding chapter often shows the audience, the question being answered, the warning being given, and the truth being revealed.

**Words of Yeshua** was built as a Christ-centred study companion for returning those words to their complete King James Version chapter context. Version 0.5.2 makes that reading experience substantially more complete.

## Begin with His words, continue into the chapter

The application provides a focused way to encounter the sayings of Jesus and then move into the larger passage around them. This is not meant to replace Scripture or to manufacture new teaching. Its role is to help the reader slow down, open the Biblical context, and follow the text itself.

Recent work completed the KJV chapter reader so that study no longer stops at an isolated selection. The surrounding verses are available as part of the same experience, making it easier to understand how a statement fits within a conversation, parable, confrontation, or moment of instruction.

That contextual emphasis is also a safeguard. Faith-oriented software should distinguish clearly between Biblical text, interface guidance, and any supporting reflection. Presentation can invite attention, but it should never blur provenance or pretend that generated words carry the authority of Scripture.

## A visual invitation to reflect

Version 0.5.2 also introduced a refreshed Hero Gallery and improved application branding. The visual work is intended to create stillness and focus, not to compete with the text. Imagery can establish a contemplative atmosphere, but Scripture remains the centre of the application.

The desktop reader is built with React and Electron and packaged for Windows. The technical foundation serves a simple purpose: offer a dedicated study space without requiring a web account or turning a reader's spiritual practice into a platform profile.

## Care before release

The v0.5.2 release was built and checked as a complete Windows application, published publicly, and then downloaded again to verify the distributed file. That process matters because faithfulness in content should be matched by care in delivery. Visitors should be able to retrieve the artifact that was actually prepared.

Words of Yeshua will continue to grow, but its direction is already firm: keep Christ at the centre, keep Scripture identifiable and in context, and use technology as a quiet aid to reading rather than a substitute for it.

> The aim is not to make the Bible sound more modern. It is to make room for the reader to hear what is already written.
