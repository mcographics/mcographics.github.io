Before the Majestic Creations website became a public home for the work, there was **Work Day with God**.

The application came first. It grew from a personal and practical desire: to keep Scripture, prayer, and attention to God present throughout the rhythm of an ordinary day—not only during a single devotional moment that is easy to leave behind once work begins.

## A devotional for the whole year

Work Day with God includes 366 Christ-centred daily devotionals, bundled KJV Scripture, reflections, prayers, and scenic imagery. The content is available offline, giving every day of the year its own place without depending on a remote service.

The application also preserves the small habits that help a reader return:

- a calendar for choosing and revisiting days;
- reading history and favourites;
- streak and progress information stored on the device;
- configurable reminders throughout the workday;
- an offline Scripture library for continued reading.

These features are not meant to turn faith into a score. They exist to support consistency and remembrance. A reminder is valuable only when it gently redirects attention toward God rather than becoming another demand for engagement.

## Free, private, and account-free

Work Day with God is completely free. It does not require an account, and its devotional history, favourites, and settings remain local to the user's device. No internet connection is required to open the day's Scripture and devotional.

That local-first design is a theological and practical choice as much as a technical one. Access to a devotional tool should not be conditioned on surrendering personal data, maintaining a subscription, or remaining connected to a company's server.

## Growing from Windows to Android

The application began as a Windows desktop experience and later expanded to Android through Capacitor. Sharing the core React application made it possible to preserve the same content and intent while respecting the differences between desktop and mobile devices.

Desktop-specific features such as the system tray, Windows notifications, file dialogs, and update handling remain behind the Electron bridge. Android uses its own native shell and notification behaviour. The result is not one platform pretending to be another, but the same devotional purpose expressed appropriately on each.

The Android 1.0.1 release was built, tested, installed on a real device, cryptographically signed, published, downloaded again, and verified. The Windows line likewise went through multiple releases as reminder scheduling, themes, transitions, support information, and branded installation behaviour improved.

## The beginning of something larger

Work Day with God predates this website, but it already contained many of the principles that would later define Majestic Creations: build something purposeful, let people own their local experience, be honest about platform boundaries, verify the release people actually receive, and treat faith-centred work with care.

The portfolio now gives the application a public place. The journal gives it something equally important—a written record that it was here before the site, quietly establishing what this studio wanted its technology to serve.

> Work Day with God was not created to replace prayer, Scripture, or fellowship. It was created to help make room for them in the middle of a busy day.
