The previous website maintenance journal was published on **August 28, 2026**, with a focus on the new Words of Yeshua product page and its place in the portfolio. Since then, the site has grown into a broader studio archive: more project pages, clearer project status, a much deeper ForgeMCP record, and better ways to move through the writing.

This update covers website work published between that August maintenance post and **October 30, 2026**. The projects themselves continue to evolve separately; this article focuses on how the site presents and organizes them.

## The project archive has grown

The homepage now presents **22 projects across four disciplines**, with seven projects marked as having an available release. Visitors can filter the archive by release availability and project category, then open a project page for more context. The archive also distinguishes released work from projects in development, research, and early building stages.

During this period, the portfolio added or expanded its presentation of Public Nuisance, FieroLink GT, ChainBreaker, Bible Recorder & Note Taker, PhotoNest, From Islam to Christ, TanyaOS, Jasper Prism, and ForgeMCP. Some arrived as new project entries; others received a dedicated page or a deeper showcase as the project developed. The site has also refreshed release details, artwork, screenshots, platform information, and journal links so that the project cards lead to useful context instead of stopping at a thumbnail.

Availability is part of that context. FieroLink GT is marked private and request-only, while release pages distinguish public downloads from platforms that do not yet have a listed build. On October 28, the Project Database entry and its release article were removed from the public portfolio when the project was no longer being presented as a public project. The [current project archive](/#work) reflects those editorial decisions.

## More focused project showcases

The site now gives [Jasper Prism](/projects/jasperprism/) its own visual identity and project experience. Its page uses an animated WebGL crystal scene for the hero, keeps its navigation and footer independent of the portfolio theme, and uses the supplied video in a restrained reveal. The effect is specific to that showcase rather than a site-wide animation.

[ForgeMCP](/projects/forgemcp/) also received a dedicated showcase under the Unreal Engine section, with its own artwork and a direct path into the development archive. The community page was connected to Discord, and the contact flow gained a request path for FieroLink GT. These additions make it easier to follow a project or ask about access without implying that private work is publicly downloadable.

## The ForgeMCP encyclopedia is now browsable

The largest archival addition is the [ForgeMCP Development Encyclopedia](/projects/forgemcp/encyclopedia/). It brings project records into a searchable, categorized reading interface with an overview, chronology, latest development updates, and links to individual records. Record pages can be opened directly and shared through their URL.

The current catalog contains **144 source Markdown records presented as 864 reading records**. Supporting evidence is reviewed into bounded reading views, and the archive labels dates as verified or inferred according to their source. Public review manifests preserve source paths, hashes, and publication dispositions. This helps readers tell the difference between a dated project record, an inferred date, and an acceptance result. Historical reports remain records of what was checked at the time; their appearance in the encyclopedia does not itself mean a later runtime or release gate has passed.

The encyclopedia was also tuned for phone reading and touch navigation. Its search covers titles, paths, categories, and summaries; record results are paginated; and the chronology and latest-update views are derived from dated catalog entries. The archive describes ForgeMCP's engineering work, including Unreal Engine and Blender activity, but those records are documentation rather than new features of this website.

## The journal is easier to browse

The blog archive now spans multiple pages instead of growing as one very long list. Category and topic archives provide additional paths into the writing, while project cards can be shared directly using their project artwork. The archive's default order is newest first.

Today, the same sort choices became available across the blog archive and the encyclopedia: **earliest to latest, latest to earliest, title A–Z, and title Z–A**. The choice applies to the relevant archive, category, topic, or encyclopedia view and is remembered in the visitor's browser when local storage is available. A reader can switch from a chronological reading order to an alphabetical one without losing that preference when moving between those archives.

The October 30 hand-rig article was also moved into the **Milestone** category so that its category page and RSS metadata describe the post consistently. The journal now records a wider range of work, from app releases and research notes to 3D production and developer-tool milestones.

## A regular maintenance check is in place

The website repository now includes a scheduled, every-other-week maintenance workflow. It runs lint, the existing site checks, and a dependency audit. When a check fails, the workflow reports the result through a GitHub issue so it can be reviewed. The workflow is diagnostic: it does not edit site content or publish a deployment.

Mobile layout refinements, contrast fixes, touch-friendly navigation, and clearer page-specific artwork have been made along the way. They support the same goal as the new archive controls: make the site easier to read and explore across its different sections.

## Where things stand

From August 28 to October 30, the website moved from a focused product-page update toward a fuller studio record. The [homepage](/) presents the current project archive; the [journal](/blog/) keeps release and development stories together; and the [ForgeMCP encyclopedia](/projects/forgemcp/encyclopedia/) gives that project's records a searchable home.

The previous maintenance post is [A Clearer Home for Words of Yeshua](/blog/site-maintenance-update-august-2026/). This update provides the next checkpoint: what has been added, how the archive has changed, and where the current published records can be explored.
